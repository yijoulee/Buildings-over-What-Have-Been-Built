  - heading: It began with an unfinished design
    body: |
      In spring 2018, a design assignment asked for a small pavilion that could convey the genius loci. The site was Zhulian in Hsinchu City, a settlement that grew around a temple between the old inner and outer city walls.

      Bricks of every age patched half-collapsed walls, and paper mulberry pushed out of cracks repaired again and again. People and place had adjusted to each other for generations, leaving the facades scarred, yet almost ornate.

      Restoring the place would end in nostalgia. Leaving it untouched would mean giving up on design. Rebuilding it as something new, however popular, would leave someone sure the mystery had been better.

      No design came. The questions became my thesis, and this site.
  - heading: About me
    body: |
      I'm Yijou Lee, an architectural designer working on the reuse of existing and historic buildings.

      I hold master’s degrees in architectural design from NYCU (Taiwan) and in the art history of Asia from Leiden University. I began in adaptive reuse and heritage design, then spent three years in BIM and digital twin development.

      Those years taught me to model buildings precisely and simulate options before any decision is made. Now I'm bringing those tools back to heritage, and I aim to do it in the Netherlands, with its long practice of herbestemming.
