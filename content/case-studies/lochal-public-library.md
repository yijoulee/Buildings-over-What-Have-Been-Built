  introduction:
    body: Built in 1932 for the Dutch National Railways (NS) in Tilburg's Spoorzone, the LocHal was the hall where locomotives and wagons were developed and repaired, employing hundreds of local workers until it shut in 2009. The shed is a single volume of roughly 90 × 60 m and 15 m high, carried by a riveted steel frame that grows lighter towards the roof and closed in by matt wired-glass facades. It is protected as a municipal monument (gemeentelijk monument).

  reuse_goal:
    body: The Municipality of Tilburg used the hall as the anchor for regenerating the railway area, a closed-off zone that had split the city in two for decades, into a mixed-use City Campus linking the historic centre with the north of Tilburg. The brief placed the Midden-Brabant public library, the arts organisations Kunstloc Brabant and Brabant C, Seats2Meet co-working, catering and event space under one roof, and asked for a library that produces knowledge as well as lending it, through labs, lectures and public events.

  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **A 15 m-high open hall is hard to heat and to make quiet**, yet subdividing it into closed rooms would erase the industrial space worth keeping. 
      - **The scale of a 90 × 60 m workshop does not suit reading and study**, so intimate places had to be found inside one vast volume.
      - **The worn industrial fabric was the heritage value**, which ruled out a polished restoration of paint, floor and steel. 
      - **The railway site was a barrier in the city**, so the building had to turn a no-go area into a destination.
  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      Rather than restoring the 1932 hangar, the design reinterprets it. The hall remains one undivided volume, and the existing steel frame sets the rhythm for every addition: landscape, furniture and textiles take the place of new rooms. 

      - The riveted steel frame, crane tracks, old paint layers and the concrete working floor with its embedded rails were kept as found, imperfections included. Reusing the structure kept new structural work to a minimum. 
      - New parts are made from a few plain materials (black steel, concrete, glass and wood) applied over large surfaces. Columns, floors, parapets and screens together trace the perpendicular axes of the former industrial process lines. 
      - Six motorised, ceiling-high textile screens by Inside Outside, totalling 4,125 m² and partly woven by the TextielMuseum Tilburg, absorb sound and can be repositioned to separate the co-working area from the library floors or to close off a small auditorium. Combined with heating zoned to where people sit and meet, they keep the hall open without filling it with closed volumes.

  solutions:
    body: "Opened in January 2019, the LocHal shows that an industrial shed can become a new library type with very little new building: the existing structure, a soft textile layer and movable furniture do most of the work. From the entrance hall, which works as a covered city square, a stair landscape climbs to quieter reading areas on the peripheral galleries and to a public city balcony (Stadsbalkon) overlooking Tilburg. The steps seat more than 1,000 people for events, and diagonal sightlines cross the entire interior. Within this frame, Mecanoo's interior adds a reading and study street lined with bookcases, study tables fitted around the old columns, a children's library and a series of themed labs. What was once a railway barrier is now a public living room for the city, shared under one roof by the library, two arts organisations and a co-working provider."
