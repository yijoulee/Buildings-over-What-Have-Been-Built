    body: |-
      The Documentation Center at the former Nazi Party Rally Grounds in Nuremberg was originally part of a monumental complex designed for Nazi Party gatherings. The complex could accommodate up to 50,000 people and included congress hall, a stadium, and parade grounds.

      Following the Second World War, architect Günther Domenig transformed part of the complex into a documentation center.

  reuse_goal:
    body: The project transforms a monumental structure associated with Nazi propaganda into a place for historical documentation and critical reflection. Rather than concealing its controversial past, the architectural intervention confronts the building's ideological legacy through a deliberate contrast between the old and the new.
  challenges:
    body: |-
      The central challenge was how to intervene in a building deeply associated with totalitarian ideology without reinforcing its original architectural language or symbolic authority.

      The new additions also needed to accommodate contemporary functions while establishing a clear distinction between the original structure and the new architectural interventions.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      Following the principle of structural independence, they establish a distinct contemporary architectural system within the historic building. In addition to the projecting entrance, the intervention introduces discussion rooms, elevator shafts, and interior walkways. 

      - The new structures are primarily supported by steel I-beams, with metal connectors embedded into the existing concrete to join the old and new construction. 
      - The new additions employ aluminum, steel, and glass to create a strong visual contrast with the original masonry surfaces. 
      - Metal cover plates are then applied over the joints to achieve a flush and seamless finish.

  solutions:
    body: |-
      The project demonstrates how architectural contrast can become a means of critically engaging with controversial heritage. Rather than pursuing material harmony or visual continuity, Domenig's intervention establishes a deliberate confrontation between historical fabric and contemporary architecture.  The new entrance that projects through the existing façade resemble a blade cutting through the ideology of far-right extremism.

      Through its independent structural system, industrial materials, and symbolic entrance, the Documentation Center transforms a former site of authoritarian propaganda into a space for historical awareness and reflection.
