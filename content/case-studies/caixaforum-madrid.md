  reuse_goal:
    body: The project aimed to transform the former industrial building into a contemporary art museum while preserving its historic brick façade. Beyond the building itself, the architects also sought to enhance the public character of the surrounding urban space by creating a more accessible and inviting ground-level environment.
  challenges:
    body: One of the major challenges was integrating the existing brick façade with the new structural system. Since the original masonry could not serve as the primary structure of the renovated building, the architects needed to preserve its historical appearance while accommodating a completely new internal structure.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      The original brick façade was carefully dismantled during construction. After the new internal floor slabs were assembled, the entire façade was lifted back into position and temporarily supported by auxiliary structures. Concrete was then poured to integrate the historic façade with the new construction. Consequently, the reassembled façade became largely non-structural, serving primarily as an architectural envelope. The original arched windows were also sealed.

      At ground level, Herzog & de Meuron introduced a folded-metal underground passage and a recessed entrance beneath the public plaza. This intervention creates the illusion of a floating brick volume while expanding the public space at street level.

  solutions:
    body: |-
      CaixaForum Madrid exemplifies a strategy of façade preservation without structural retention, allowing:

      - Flexibility in reorganizing the interior and the possiblity of recessed entrance
      - The new structural system that consists of three internal concrete cores, which also accommodate vertical circulation and building services. 

      Meanwile, the uppermost floor combines steel framing and concrete slabs, enclosed by a perforated weathering-steel curtain wall with varying degrees of openness to address different lighting privacy requirements.

      Rather than simply restoring the former factory, Herzog & de Meuron transform it into a floating urban artifact, where history serves as a symbolic skin while contemporary architecture defines the building’s performance and civic presence.
