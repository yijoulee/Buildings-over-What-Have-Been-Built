  introduction:
    body: "Delft's former armamentarium stands on a narrow, wedge-shaped strip of land where the Oude Delft and Lange Geer canals meet at the Schie, with water on three sides. It is a row of warehouses grouped around courtyards: the white building of 1602 at the western tip, set around a small courtyard, and a second, larger building of 1692 further east, around a wide courtyard, with a gatehouse building between the two. A former VOC warehouse of 1653 adjoins the complex and had already been converted into offices by cepezed. For four centuries the buildings stored weapons and munitions, the Ministry of War moved in after 1948, and from 1989 to 2013 the Legermuseum opened them to the public. Even then the site could be reached by land only along the narrow Korte Geer quay, so it remained an island in the city"

  reuse_goal:
    body: "When the Legermuseum left Delft, the developer Driestar bought the buildings in 2015 and, together with cepezedprojects, won the plan to turn them into a publicly accessible, multi-tenant destination: an exhibition hall, a restaurant with conference rooms, and a hotel. The heritage stance was to keep the complex's characteristic exterior and original structure, to limit interventions to what the new uses needed, and to make every addition reversible and clearly recognisable as new. A second aim was to tie the closed military enclave into the surrounding streets and canals, so that the quays could become public space."

  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **An island in the city:** ​with canals on three sides and a single narrow land access, the complex needed new connections, and any bridge had to land without loading the protected quay walls.
      - **Untouchable, irregular structure:** a 3D laser scan in 2017 showed that floors and walls were far from level and plumb, while the brief allowed no changes to the load-bearing structure, so hotel rooms and service shafts could only go where the existing beams and window openings permitted. 
      - **Neglected listed fabric:** after decades of minimal upkeep, rotten timber, flaking plaster, worn paintwork, roofs and gutters all had to be repaired before any new tenant could move in.
  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      
      The project worked through restoration and reversible plug-in: the shell was repaired first, during the three-year zoning-plan procedure, the new uses were added as removable modular elements that leave the historic timber structure in view, and new openings and bridges turned the inward-looking complex towards the city. -

      - **Two footbridges:** slender, dark-grey steel bridges spanning about 13 m and 16 m link the complex to the Oude Delft and the Lange Geer. Their decks were prefabricated and lifted in from a pontoo, and they rest on hidden screwed tubular piles in the canal bed, so the monumental quays carry no load. A floating terrace extends the restaurant onto the water.
      - **Repaired and reopened envelope:** the neglected shell was restored first, during the three-year zoning-plan procedure. Bricked-up windows on the courtyards and closed shutters along the Korte Geer were then opened again, and the former closed gateways received glass fronts with slim frames, so activity inside is visible from the street. New elements are deliberately modern, aiming for harmony through contrast.
      - **Glass roof over the 1602 courtyard:** a new glazed roof covers the small courtyard of the white building, turning the open yard into a covered patio that adds dining space to the restaurant and becomes the interior centre of the building. 
      - **Plug-in hotel:** the 63 rooms are assembled from loose modular elements placed within the existing timber beam structure, which stays largely visible. Their layout follows the existing beams and daylight openings, and shafts were added only where the structure allowed. The corridors run along the line of timber posts, which stand free between the wood-clad walls of the room modules. Bathrooms arrived as factory-made, self-supporting units of about 2 × 3 m or 3 × 3 m, with a steel frame and seamless coated-steel interior, craned in sealed and connected through a grouped service recess. They can later be removed or moved. 
      - **Beams closed and opened:** some rooms are closed with a new ceiling of their own, which completes the room module and screens it from the floor above, while other rooms, the corridors and the common areas on the hotel floors keep the beams in view. In one common area the ceiling is left open up into the roof structure, so a roof window brings daylight down into the space.

  solutions:
    body: After construction from 2018 to 2022, ArsenaalDelft was put into use in July 2022. Quays opened to the public and new routes through the inner city via the two bridges. Now the 1602 white building holds the restaurant and conference centre, the gatehouse building between the two was transformed into the entrance building, and the 1692 building takes the exhibition space and a shop on the ground floor with the hotel on the two upper floors. After four centuries as a closed military site, the complex has become a public part of Delft's historic centre, and it shows how a dense hotel programme can be plugged into a listed structure without altering it.
