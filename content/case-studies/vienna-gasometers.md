  introduction:
    body: The four gasholder houses were built between 1896 and 1899 as part of the municipal gasworks in Simmering, Vienna's 11th district, to plans by Theodor Herrmann. All four are identical. A domed concrete slab 1.70 m thick carries a 12 m high brick ring, 62.80 m in inner diameter, that served as the water basin. Above it rise brick walls about 70 m high, tapering from 5.40 m thick at the base to 0.90 m near the top, crowned by a self-supporting iron Schwedler dome with a 63.60 m span. Inside, a three-ring telescopic steel bell holding up to 90,000 m³ of gas rose and fell on 18 lattice guides. Arched windows and crenellated parapets dressed the plant up as civic architecture. Once the city switched from town gas to natural gas (1969–1978), the holders became obsolete and were retired in the mid-1980s.

  reuse_goal:
    body: In the mid-1990s the City of Vienna chose to build housing inside its historic building stock, after years of rapid subsidised construction on the city's edge. The gasometers were to provide good-quality but affordable flats, financed through Vienna's housing subsidy system, together with shops, offices and services that would make them a self-contained quarter. They were also meant to spark the development of the surrounding Erdberger Mais district, and an extension of the U3 metro was a precondition. The brick envelopes were to stay, with as few changes as possible, and the gutted interiors were to be rebuilt. One cylinder was assigned to each of four architectural teams under Wehdorn's masterplan.

  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **Protection of the original facade and limited construction space**: every new use had to fit inside fixed cylinders about 63 m across, and each new opening meant cutting carefully into protected brickwork. 
      - **Excavation through hard masonry**: the masonry of the basin rings was as hard as reinforced concrete and needed mining equipment, in ground with a high water table. 
      - **Acoustic and vibration isolation**: an event hall for up to about 4,200 people had to sit at the base of Gasometer B, directly below a student hostel and flats, without passing on noise or vibration. 
      - **Coherence across four separate designs**: a shared framework was needed so that four very different infill schemes would still work as one connected district.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      The project worked through infill: the brick cylinders were kept as an outer skin, the original structure was reused where possible, and four different building-within-a-building schemes were placed inside on a shared commercial base. 

      - **Vertical zoning**: about 1,000 parking spaces fill the basements and lower floors. Above them, a two-level mall runs through all four cylinders at the former entrance level, linked by bridges, with offices above and flats starting only at about 30 m. 
      - **Reused structure**: in C and D, the existing base slabs and footings were used as flat foundations for the new concrete structure. The restored Schwedler domes were left uncovered as open steel wFebs, which lets light into the courtyards; they were wind-tunnel tested beforehand so they would not whistle in strong wind. 
      - **Gasometer A (Nouvel)**: nine paired wedge-shaped segments of 14 storeys stand slightly back from the brick wall to leave a vertical gap for circulation. Their sides are clad in reflective glass and polished metal, which multiplies the light, and a glass dome covers the central shopping atrium. 
      - **Gasometer B (Coop Himmelb(l)au)**: student housing and flats ring a conical inner courtyard. Outside the cylinder, an angular "shield" slab with 116 flats and offices rises above the brick parapet on raking legs. The event hall at the base is a separate concrete shell on rubber bearings, with no structural connection to the cylinder, to block sound. 
      - **Gasometer C (Wehdorn**): a ring of housing against the old wall is divided into six tower-like wings that terrace back towards the top, around a densely planted courtyard shaped like an inverted cone. Only narrow new slits were cut, and the flats also have opening windows to the outside. 
      - **Gasometer D (Holzbauer)**:a star-shaped plan cut by three smaller atriums replaces a single central void, and loggias face these open spaces. The lower floors hold the Vienna City and Provincial Archive, with flats on eight upper floors and a kindergarten in the southern atrium.

  solutions:
    body: Completed in summer 2001, Gasometer City showed early on that monumental industrial shells can carry dense, affordable mixed-use housing. About 840 homes including student units, about 11,000 m² of offices, around 20,000 m² of retail, the city archive and an event hall for more than 4,000 people. Because four architects answered the same brief inside four identical shells, the project is a rare side-by-side comparison of infill strategies.
