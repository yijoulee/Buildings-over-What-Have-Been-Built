  introduction:
    body: Het Arsenaal is a municipal monument in Leiden, formed by two adjoining square blocks, each enclosing an inner courtyard. Built in the early 19th century as the arsenal of the Doelenkazerne barracks, it was one of the few structures spared when most of the military complex was demolished in the early 1980s. A first conversion by Tjeerd Dijkstra rebuilt the western wings in a historicising style, roofed the west courtyard with a timber-and-glass structure and filled the east courtyard with a library, turning the building into a home for the university's Faculty of Arts from 1981.

  reuse_goal:
    body: The 2019–2020 renovation aimed to turn a dated, inward-looking faculty building into contemporary workplaces, teaching rooms and study spaces shaped by openness and transparency. At the same time, the university wanted the monument to become fully gas-free and to reach at least a BREEAM-NL "Very Good" rating, without compromising its authentic 19th-century character.
  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **A protected envelope limited energy performance**, as the building outline and floor area had to remain unchanged. 
      - **New climate systems had to stay invisible**, without altering the historic timber beams or the roofscape. 
      - **Earlier interventions had closed the building off**, with the filled-in east courtyard cutting daylight from the lower floors. 
      - **Openness versus privacy**: staff preferred enclosed offices over a fully open plan.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      Rather than adding new volumes, the architects worked through subtraction and concealment. 

      - Removing the library's book tower reopened the east courtyard as an inner garden with a glass walkway and green walls, bringing daylight and sightlines back across several levels. 
      - In the west courtyard, Dijkstra's timber structure was kept as part of the building's layered history, topped with a new highly insulated solar-control glass roof. Beneath it, a study landscape gathers around a monolithic table for about 100 students, while a grey cast floor traces the outline of the original courtyards. 
      - All facade windows were replaced with double glazing set in 19th-century profiles, so the exterior reads as unchanged. 
      - Heating, cooling and acoustic absorption come from plastered capillary climate ceilings fitted between the original beams. Air-handling units are hidden in the attic, supported by heat pumps and rooftop PV. 
      - Through circular dismantling, Oregon pine from the old window frames became stairs and toilet walls, courtyard cobblestones were relaid in the garden, and other components were passed on to other buildings.

  solutions:
    body: The Arsenaal shows how a monument can meet contemporary standards by working within its existing shell rather than beyond it. The exterior keeps its historic appearance, while the building now runs gas-free. The west courtyard, converted into a study hall, has become the building’s spatial and visual core. Completed in March 2020, the Arsenaal now houses the LUCAS and LUCL research institutes.
