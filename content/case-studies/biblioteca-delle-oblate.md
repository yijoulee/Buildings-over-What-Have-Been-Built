  introduction:
    body: "The Oblate complex grew out of the hospital Folco Portinari founded in 1285–1288, the first core of Ospedale di Santa Maria Nuova. When a new men's ward was built across the square in the early 14th century, the old site became the women's ward, and convent quarters were added for the Oblate, a lay community of women who nursed female patients and did the cooking, weaving and laundry for the whole hospital. The mostly 15th-century wings are arranged around two open spaces: a cloister whose three-tier loggia starts with a ground floor of 1329, with \\*pietra forte\\* pillars and shield capitals, and a mid-15th-century garden framed on two sides by a portico of \\*pietra serena\\* Ionic columns. On the roof, the former laundry-drying rooms form an \\*altana\\* facing the Duomo. In 1936 the City of Florence bought the empty complex and, after works in 1938–1941, filled it with museums, cultural institutes and the municipal Biblioteca Comunale Centrale."

  reuse_goal:
    body: 'After bringing the whole building up to current safety codes between 1999 and 2002, the city decided in 2002 to use part of the complex for a new, modern public library, funded by the Comune and the Regione Toscana. The Biblioteca Comunale Centrale, a specialist library of Florentine and Tuscan history, was to become an open reading library and cultural centre with free services for all ages: open shelves, study places, rooms for children and families, meeting rooms and a café, open into the evening. The convent rooms, cloisters and courtyards were to be kept and opened to the public, while the academy, the prehistory museum and the Risorgimento library stayed in the complex.'

  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **Services in historic room**s: the convent halls around the main cloister had to take air conditioning, data networks, alarms, CCTV and anti-theft systems while keeping their historic interiors legible. 
      - **Vertical circulation**: the upper floors needed a lift and a second escape route, and the new lift could only be built by moving many existing installations. 
      - **A closed back-of-house**: the entrance on Via dell'Oriuolo was a narrow opening onto an asphalted courtyard used for parking, so the complex turned its back on the street.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      The project worked through restoration and integration: the convent rooms were kept largely as found, the new building services were built into furniture-like insertions, and a second phase opened the complex to the street. 

      - **A new front door**: the Via dell'Oriuolo entrance was widened, the asphalted courtyards behind it were recovered as a central piazza joined by ramps with planters, and the connecting cloister between this piazza and the 15th-century cloister was restored. 
      - **Programme by floor around the cloister**: the historic collection, reception, children's section and conference rooms sit on the ground floor, the contemporary open-shelf collection on the first floor, and study rooms and the café on the second, all wrapped around the main cloister.
      - **Integrated building services**: in the three large first-floor halls and the second-floor hall facing the main cloister, a dense network of ducts and pipes runs inside plasterboard structures that double as the shelving framework. They set out the winding line of the shelves and carry the lighting, so the convent walls stay untouched. In a reading hall under the timber roof trusses, large air ducts instead run openly beneath the roof. 
      - **New vertical circulation**: a fire escape stair linking the first and second floors was inserted into a rear cloister, and a lift with a glazed panoramic cabin was added.

  solutions:
    body: The Biblioteca delle Oblate opened on 25 May 2007 and turned a closed institutional building into a public meeting place in the centre of Florence. It shows how a conversion run by the city's own heritage office can fit a modern library into historic rooms almost without touching them, by building the services into the furniture and opening back courtyards as a new entrance. Further works funded by the Ente Cassa di Risparmio from 2011 brought more rooms into use.
