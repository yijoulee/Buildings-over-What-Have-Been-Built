  introduction:
    body: |-
      The Church of San Lorenzo in Miranda occupies the former Temple of Antoninus and Faustina, originally dedicated in 141 CE in the Roman Forum. Situated within the political and civic centre of ancient Rome, the temple was surrounded by significant monuments, including the Arch of Constantine and the Temple of Castor and Pollux.

      The temple was converted into a Christian church in 630, when Pope Honorius II established a church dedicated to Saint Lawrence within its ruins. This medieval church remained in use until 1429, when Pope Martin V suppressed it and transferred the complex to the city's apothecaries' guild, the Universitas Aromatariorum Urbis (Bianchini et al., 2025).

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      The current church was commissioned by the Nobile Collegio degli Speziali and designed by architect Orazio Torriani. Construction began in 1602, with the main building completed in 1616. Its upper façade was later completed by Matteo Sassi between 1721 and 1722 (Bianchini et al., 2025).

      Rather than demolishing the ancient temple, Torriani integrated the new church into its surviving structure. The Roman temple's raised base, side walls and front colonnade were retained.  Rather than replacing the ancient frontage, the new church wall and pediment were set back behind the existing columns, creating a layered façade in which the original Roman portico remained visible in front of the later church structure.

The resulting layered façade makes the relationship between the building's Roman origins and its subsequent Christian use clearly legible.
