  introduction:
    body: The building beside the Kattendijkdok began as Antwerp’s fire station, completed in 1922 to a design by city architect Emiel Van Averbeke. Its style imitates the palazzo-like Hanseatic House that once stood where the MAS is today. The building is divided into four parts, each under its own roof, around an open courtyard where hoses were once tested, and its interior rests on columns rather than load-bearing walls. The decorated hose-drying tower shown on the plans was never finished and stopped at about 20 m. The building was listed in 2000, and it stood empty after a new fire station replaced it.

  reuse_goal:
    body: "By 2007 the Port of Antwerp had outgrown its 1990s offices and decided to move. It wanted one sustainable, future-proof headquarters that would bring together roughly 500 technical and administrative staff who were spread across several sites in the city, laid out as an activity-based office. The Flemish Government Architect's team ran the competition with the City and the Port. It had a single rule: the fire station had to be preserved."
  challenges:
    body: |-
      - **Preservation was non-negotiable**: the listed building could not be demolished, and its historic facades were to stay unobscured. 
      - **The old building could not hold the programme on its own**: about 500 workplaces needed roughly twice the floor area the fire station offered. 
      - **There was no back side**: all four elevations carried equal weight, so an addition next to the building would have hidden at least one of them.

  adaptive_strategy:
    body: |-
      ZHA worked by superposition. Instead of building alongside the fire station, the new office floats above its roof and reads as a belated completion of the tower that was never finished.

      - **Courtyard roofed in glass**: the fire station's central courtyard became the main reception atrium. The glass roof is fixed into the old anchor holes above the windows, which once held beams for fire crews working on the roof and facade. The former fire-truck hall was restored as a public reading room and library. 
      - **Elevated glass volume**: a five-storey extension over 100 m long, roughly the length of the building below, rises to 46 m on concrete supports and about 900 tonnes of steel. At one end it cantilevers past the dormer roof onto a tapered concrete leg that holds an observation point, and its prow points north towards the Scheldt. 
      - **Faceted skin**: the extension is clad in flat triangular glass panels, smooth at the south end and increasingly rippled towards the north. Transparent and opaque panels are mixed to balance daylight, solar control and views, and the facets allude to Antwerp's diamond trade. 
      - **Shared functions at the seam**: a circulation tower runs up through the centre of the old building into the extension, supplemented by panoramic lifts and an external bridge. The restaurant, meeting rooms and a 90-seat auditorium sit where old and new meet, with open-plan offices on the remaining floors.

  solutions:
    body: Port House opened in September 2016. It shows that a listed building can be kept whole, with every facade visible, when the new floor area goes above it rather than around it, and when the shared programme is placed at the junction so that the old and new parts work as one.
